Approval is a moment. Exposure continues.

The ETA CPP framework treats Risk, Regulatory/Compliance/Security and Underwriting as distinct professional domains.

1 · Authorization

The sale is approved

Funds or credit appear available and the transaction is allowed to continue.

2 · Fulfillment

Value is released

The merchant provides goods or services before every downstream risk is resolved.

3 · Settlement

Funds move

The acquiring side funds the merchant based on the expectation that the transaction remains valid.

4 · After the sale

Liability remains

Fraud, refunds, disputes and chargebacks can still change the outcome.

KYC & Underwriting

Know the merchant before the merchant is boarded.

KYC (Know Your Customer) and underwriting confirm who the merchant is, what the business actually does and whether the application accurately reflects the risk being presented.

What underwriting reviews

Business details, ownership, processing history, expected monthly volume, average ticket and supporting documentation.

Why it asks

Volume and ticket size help establish the merchant's expected risk profile. Missing or inaccurate information can delay approval and create risk after boarding.

Your role as a Pilot

Collect accurate, complete information and never code around the truth. If an account looks prohibited, high-risk or outside the normal profile, stop and escalate.

Try it Out! Explain to a merchant why underwriting asks for their expected monthly volume and average ticket, in a way that makes the question feel reasonable rather than intrusive.

Transaction & Data Controls

PCI DSS: protecting the card data behind every sale.

PCI DSS is the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard. It sets the security expectations for any business that stores, processes or transmits cardholder data.

What it protects

The environment where cardholder data is stored, processed or transmitted, including terminals, software, networks and the people who handle card information.

Why it matters after approval

Fraud tools evaluate a transaction in the moment. PCI DSS protects the data before, during and after it, so an approved sale does not become exposure later.

Your role as a Pilot

Encourage secure processing practices and set accurate expectations. For PCI questions beyond what you know, bring in the right internal resource.

Try it Out! In one or two sentences, explain to a merchant why PCI DSS matters to their business without using the words compliance or standard.

Ongoing Monitoring

Approval sets the baseline. Monitoring watches for change.

A merchant's risk profile does not stop at activation. Volume, ticket size, refunds and chargebacks continue to matter for as long as the merchant processes.

What gets watched

Processing volume, ticket size, refunds and chargebacks compared with the profile the merchant was approved on.

Why it matters

A sudden jump in ticket size or volume can mean the merchant's actual processing no longer matches the expected profile and needs another look.

Your role as a Pilot

The numbers you collect during the sale become the baseline. If you learn a merchant's business or processing is changing, bring in the right internal resource.

Try it Out! Your merchant was approved on a $40 average ticket and now runs several $900 transactions in one week. What questions would you ask, and who would you bring in?
