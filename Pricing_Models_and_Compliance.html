Before We Get Into Pricing

Dodd-Frank & the Durbin Amendment

Dual Pricing didn't start as a payments product, it started at the gas pump. Here's how a decades-old workaround for surcharge restrictions turned into the compliant model you sell today, and why compliance has been the deciding factor at every step along the way.

1970s
During the oil crisis, gas stations pioneer cash discounting, posting a lower price for cash to offset rising card costs and protect thin fuel margins. This is where the practice starts, a workaround at the pump, not a product.
1981
The Cash Discount Act amends Truth in Lending, legally drawing the line between a "discount" and a "surcharge." It turns the gas-station workaround into a codified, compliant model, the direct root of today's Dual Pricing.
1984
The original federal surcharge ban lapses. Surcharging becomes a state-by-state patchwork while cash discounting stays legal nationwide, which is exactly why it became the dominant path for the next three decades.
2010
The Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform Act passes. Tucked inside it: the Durbin Amendment, capping debit interchange for large banks and mandating competitive debit routing, reshaping the cost pressure every pricing program is built to offset.
2013
A Visa/Mastercard settlement opens the door nationally for credit-only surcharge programs, giving merchants a second compliant path alongside cash discounting.
Today
Compliant POS technology, BIN lookup, card-price-as-posted-price systems, automates what gas stations once did by hand. What started as a pump-side workaround is now the Dual Pricing program you sell.
Bottom line: cash discounting was a compliance workaround before it was ever a sales pitch. That's still true today, every Dual Pricing rule traces back to staying on the legal side of the discount versus surcharge line.

Merchant Pays

Interchange Plus (IC+)

Every transaction = interchange rate (set by Visa/MC) + your processor's fixed markup. Both appear separately on the statement. The most transparent model and the easiest to justify with a side-by-side comparison.

How to Read a Statement

Push for itemized IC+ statements. Bundled statements hide costs. Itemized lets you spot card mix issues and B2B optimization opportunities.

Card Mix Matters

Rewards, corporate, and premium cards carry higher interchange, set by the card networks, not the processor. This is your opening to talk about cost offsets.

Compliance note: Durbin only caps debit interchange. Credit interchange isn't federally capped, it's set by the networks and varies by card type, which is exactly why card mix matters here.

Best for: Merchants wanting full visibility, professional services, high-volume retail, B2B.

Merchant Offsets

Dual Pricing

Merchant marks up listed prices to the "card price." Cash customers are discounted back down, up to 4%. The card price IS the advertised price. No fee added at point of sale.

What Customers See

Card price and cash price displayed side-by-side at checkout. No surprise fee — that's the key distinction from surcharging.

Compliance Requirements

  • Card price must be the price posted and advertised, not a separate add-on
  • Both prices must be clearly displayed together at checkout
  • Legal in all 50 states, unlike surcharging, which several states restrict

Compatible Platforms

Valor PayTech (VP100, VP550, VP800) and SwipeSimple (A920 Pro, A80). Specify cash discount % in your deployment request notes.

Compliance note: this rests on the 1981 Cash Discount Act, which drew the legal line between a "discount" and a "surcharge." Because it's structured as a discount, no separate fee is ever shown at checkout.

Best for: Retail, QSR, food trucks, any merchant with fixed posted pricing.

Customer Pays

Surcharge Program

A percentage fee (1–3%) added at checkout for credit card transactions only. BIN lookup on compliant devices auto-recognizes and excludes debit automatically.

Compliance Requirements

  • Posted signage required before the sale
  • Must apply at all locations, no cherry-picking
  • Not available in CT or MA

Card Network Rules

Applied to Visa, MC, Discover, and AMEX OptBlue credit only. Surcharge never applies to debit or prepaid — BIN lookup handles this automatically.

Compliance note: credit-only surcharging became possible after a 2013 Visa/Mastercard settlement, subject to state law. Debit stays excluded because its fee and routing protections are federal law under Durbin, not a program rule.

Best for: Credit-heavy card mix, merchants wanting full fee elimination.
